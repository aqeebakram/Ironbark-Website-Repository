Why Ironbark

I didn't read about this career. I lived it, on both sides of the desk.

A lot of people in this market are selling a country they've never actually lived in. I have, for twelve years, as a student, an employee, and eventually someone sitting in the room where the policy got written.

01

Arrived as an international student myself

I landed in Australia in 2016, already used to working with Australian clients from a distance. Didn't matter. The first few months still knocked me sideways, new place, new people, a completely different way of doing things. I remember wishing someone could just tell me how it actually worked, instead of figuring it out alone.

02

Worked inside Australian higher education, at Macquarie University and the Australian National University

At Macquarie I was the student advisor running GTE checks and helping new arrivals actually settle in, not just enrol. At ANU I moved from recruitment officer to running the international recruitment team, which meant learning admissions, faculties, and research areas from the inside, not off a prospectus.

03

Led teams in professional Australian settings

Running a recruitment function meant working with students and institutions from all over the world, and the one thing that stuck: there's no single right answer. Every student, every institution, needed advice built around their actual situation, not a template.

04

Served in government, at ministerial and diplomatic level

I managed international engagement and partnerships for the New South Wales Government's trade and investment arm, connecting universities, schools, and vocational providers with international opportunities, and running the programs that support international students actually living and studying in the state.

05

Now based in the UAE, building the bridge families actually need

After a decade in Australia, I wanted a new challenge, and Australian citizenship meant I could go looking for one. I've sat in boardrooms with a state minister, vice-chancellors, and foreign governments, and I've sat across from a nervous student figuring out their first move abroad. Student, university staff, public servant, I've seen this from every side of the table now.
